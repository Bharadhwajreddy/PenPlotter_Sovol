## 0. Executive summary

1. **Start without the money rail.** On day 1, launch only the parts that need no licence. These are pitch videos and content, the mentor marketplace, business verification, franchise-brand lead generation, and referrals to bank and MUDRA loans. Turn on community lending through an NBFC-P2P partner only after a written legal opinion. The RBI's 2024 P2P amendment bars matching "within a closed user group", and it gives "borrowers/lenders sourced through an affiliate/service provider to the NBFC-P2P" as an example **[S30]**. A platform that sources both the businesses and their backers sits right next to that line.
2. **Supply first, through franchise brands.** Franchise brands are the paying side, and each brand brings many aspiring franchisees. Give those aspirants single-player value first: a franchise-readiness kit, a verified profile, a pitch-video builder, mentor feedback and MUDRA/JanSamarth help. This follows the "come for the tool, stay for the network" pattern **[S45][S46]** and supply-first marketplace practice **[S41][S42]**.
3. **One city, made dense.** The recommended first city is **Greater Warangal (Warangal–Hanamkonda–Kazipet), Telangana**. Vijayawada (AP) is the second city, to be prepared from month 10.
4. **The event is the content engine.** Run a monthly "Fish Tank Live" pitch night and turn each one into Telugu YouTube episodes and reels. Josh Talks ran events in 75+ Tier-II/III cities before pivoting to content-first in 2017 **[S20]**. Shark Tank India brands report big but short-lived traffic spikes after airing **[S17]**.
5. **The demand-side loop comes from the borrower's own network.** This is the Kiva "private fundraising" and Honeycomb "your own customers" mechanic **[S38][S39]**. It is adapted to Indian P2P rules only if counsel confirms it is allowed.

## 2. Cold start: what the playbooks and examples say

**Principles**
- Constrain the market by geography or category. Win the constrained side first, usually supply. Most of the benefit comes from 2–3 levers **[S41][S42]**.
- Build the "atomic network", the smallest network that can sustain itself. Attract the "hard side" first, often with subsidy **[S43]**.
- Give the product "single-player mode" value before network effects kick in **[S45]**. "Come for the tool, stay for the network" **[S46]**.

**Indian marketplace examples**
- **Urban Company:** The founders split ownership of the sides, with one founder on supply. They manually recruited professionals in Delhi NCR, including village visits, and made the professional app mandatory early. Training academies became a supply moat **[S25]**.
- **Zomato (Foodiebay):** Pure supply seeding. The founders collected and scanned menus by hand, starting on the Bain intranet. They launched with about 1,200 Delhi NCR restaurants in July 2008 **[S26]** **[VERIFY count]**.
- **NoBroker:** Supply first (owners). "Click and earn" paid ₹100 for a listing photo, credited with about a third of new listings **[S27]** **[VERIFY]**. It went city by city.

**Lending / community-finance examples**
- **Kiva U.S.:** "Social underwriting". The borrower first raises from their own friends and family in a private period of up to 15 days, with 5–50 lenders depending on loan size. Only then does the loan go public. Alternatively a Trustee can vouch **[S38]**.
- **Honeycomb Credit (US):** Local businesses borrow from "their own loyal customers, fans and community". Requirements were ≥6 months of operation and $10k–$100k raises, with a $250 listing fee and a free campaign video **[S39]**. Honeycomb reports more than $23M deployed via more than 23,000 investments **[S39]** **[VERIFY]**.
- **Rang De / IndiaP2P (India):** Local partner organisations verify and support borrowers in person **[S16][S40]**. IndiaP2P's FY25 report: 78% of loans to women, >99% non-urban **[S40]**.

**Implication for Fish Tank [ASSUMPTION/strategy]**
- **Hard side = credible, verified pitchers.** Franchise brands can supply them in bulk and pay for outcomes.
- **Money side, phase 1:** Use institutional money that already exists, such as MUDRA via banks **[S35]**, JanSamarth **[S36]** and Telangana's Indira Mahila Shakti (IMS) SHG loans **[S31]**. Backers are not needed to give pitchers an outcome.
- **Money side, phase 2:** Community backers seeded Kiva-style from each pitcher's own circle. This requires legal clearance (Section 4).
- **Constrain the market three ways:** one city, one category (franchise launches) and one ticket band (≤ ₹10 lakh).

| Candidate | Why | Facts |

| **LiquiLoans** | Proven embedded partnerships | Powers CRED Mint. CRED took a minority stake in 2022 **[S28]** |
| **Faircent** | MSME loans, co-lending product | Launched "Faircent Pro" co-lending in 2022 **[S29]** **[VERIFY current]** |

**P2P rules that shape the product** (RBI NBFC-P2P Master Direction as amended 16 Aug 2024) **[S30]**:
- Lender aggregate exposure across all P2P platforms ≤ ₹50 lakh. Above ₹10 lakh needs a CA net-worth certificate showing ≥ ₹50 lakh **[S30]**.
- Single lender → single borrower ≤ ₹50,000 across platforms. Borrower aggregate ≤ ₹10 lakh. Tenor ≤ 36 months **[S30b]** **[VERIFY against current RBI text]**.
  - So a ₹5 lakh franchise loan needs at least 10 backers [arithmetic].
  - Amul-type parlours (about ₹1.85–5 lakh, per blogs **[S32]** **[VERIFY]**) fit P2P limits.
  - Pharmacy franchises (MedPlus about ₹15–20 lakh **[S33]** **[VERIFY, 2016 data]**) need a bank or MUDRA loan.
- Funds flow only through two bank-trustee escrow accounts, with **T+1**. No cash **[S30]**. Fish Tank never touches money.
- **No credit enhancement or guarantee**, no assured returns or liquidity options, and **no promoting P2P as an investment product** **[S30]**.
- **Closed user group matching is not permitted.** Example given: borrowers/lenders sourced through an affiliate/service provider to the NBFC-P2P **[S30]**.
- Fees must be a fixed amount or a fixed proportion of principal and cannot depend on repayment. Pricing cannot be outsourced **[S30][S30c]**.
- Explicit lender risk declaration **[S30]**. Monthly NPA disclosure and disclosure of lender losses **[S30d]**.
- Enforcement precedent: the RBI fined two P2P platforms in 2024, partly for routing repayments through an LSP's nodal account **[S30e]**.

### 3.2 Lending Service Provider (LSP) rules: RBI Digital Lending Directions, 2025 (8 May 2025)
- An LSP is an agent of a regulated entity (RE) that performs digital lending functions such as sourcing, underwriting support or servicing **[S34][S34b]**.
- Disbursal and repayment must go directly between the borrower and the RE. No LSP pass-through or pool accounts **[S34b]**.
- KFS before contract. At least 1-day cooling-off. Grievance officers at both the RE and the LSP **[S34b]**.
- Data: minimum necessary, explicit consent, stored in India, no biometrics **[S34b]**.
- Multi-lender LSPs must show *all* matching offers, with APR and a KFS link. No dark patterns or selective promotion **[S34b]**. One source dates the multi-lender clause to 1 Nov 2025 **[S34]** **[VERIFY]**.
- REs must report DLAs (including LSP apps) on RBI CIMS **[S34b]**.
- DLG ≤ 5% of portfolio, only with conditions **[S34b]**. **[ASSUMPTION]** Fish Tank should offer no DLG at all. It is prohibited on P2P anyway.
- **Implication:** if Fish Tank refers borrowers digitally to banks or NBFCs, it is their LSP. Each RE must do due diligence on it and report its app. Budget for the compliance work.
- **[VERIFY]** Whether the Digital Lending Directions apply to NBFC-P2P on top of the P2P Directions was not settled in the sources. Assume they do.

### 3.4 Government credit rails Fish Tank can route pitchers to (no licence needed to help them apply)
- **PMMY/MUDRA:** Shishu ≤ ₹50k, Kishor ≤ ₹5 lakh, Tarun ≤ ₹10 lakh. Tarun Plus goes up to ₹20 lakh for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan, effective 24 Oct 2024 **[S35]**.

- **Udyam Assist** for informal micro-enterprises: no documents, priority-sector status. About 70% of registrations are women-owned (Jan 2025, SIDBI) **[S44]**. Use it to formalise pitchers.
- **Bank referral economics:** DSA payouts are cited at about 0.5–2% of disbursed amount for business loans (DSA-platform blogs) **[S47]** **[VERIFY with banks]**.

**Design consequences [ASSUMPTION]**
- Use the words "back / lend", never "invest".
- Never show IRR or return claims in creatives.
- The backer's money goes straight to the P2P escrow. Fish Tank deep-links to the partner's KYC and lending flow.
- Ideally, listings appear on the partner's open marketplace, not a Fish Tank-only pool. The aim is to avoid a closed user group.
- Fish Tank's fee from the partner is contractual and never repayment-linked, and the partner sets participant pricing **[S30]**.

**Option to test with counsel**
- Fish Tank sources *borrowers only* (pitchers), and the P2P's general lender base funds them.
- Fish Tank-referred backers onboard directly with the partner and may lend to any borrower.
- If counsel says even this is risky, run "Fish Tank Backing" only as a pledge or interest signal. The actual lending then happens openly on the partner platform, and the pitcher's bank/MUDRA loan remains the main route.

### 7.4 Top 10 risks and mitigations

| # | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The P2P structure is judged a "closed user group"** **[S30]** | Get a counsel opinion before launch. List on the partner's open marketplace. Source borrowers only if needed. Bank/MUDRA rail as the primary route **[S35][S36]** |
| 2 | **Marketing reads as an "investment" or promises returns** **[S30]** | Copy rules and pre-approval by the partner's compliance team. Words: "back/lend". No return figures. Train creators and judges. Treat SEBI's finfluencer rules as a guide **[S57]** |
| 3 | **Pitchers or community members collect money off-platform** (BUDS) **[S61]** | ToS ban, moderation of WhatsApp groups, in-app warnings. No pooled pledges outside escrow |
| 4 | **Defaults hurt backers and the brand** | Small tickets (₹50k cap **[S30b]**), diversification nudges **[S19]**, brand plus field-partner due diligence (Rang De/IndiaP2P model **[S16][S40]**), mentor check-ins, transparent loss disclosure **[S30d]** |
| 5 | **Cold start: too few backers** | Pitchers still get value from readiness, mentors, brands and bank loans without backers. Kiva-style own-network seeding (if allowed) **[S38]** |
| 6 | **Bad or fraudulent franchise brands** | Admit only brands with operating outlets. Disclosure checklist. Reference calls with existing franchisees. Fees paid only on verified opening [ASSUMPTION] |
| 7 | **IP and brand conflict** (Shark Tank similarity; clip rights) | Trademark search and distinct identity. Fish Tank owns footage and grants pitchers reuse rights **[S17b]** |
| 8 | **Data-protection breach or non-compliance** (DPDP from about May 2027; DL data rules) **[S34b][S58]** | Privacy by design, India hosting, consented verification. No Aadhaar authentication without approval **[S59]** |
| 9 | **Partner or regulatory shock** (RBI tightened P2P in 2024 and fined platforms **[S30][S30e]**) | Two P2P partners plus bank rails. Contract exit clauses. Watch RBI circulars |
| 10 | **Field-incentive gaming and CAC creep** **[S8]** | Pay Mitras on verified outcomes with clawbacks. Cohort-level CAC tracking. Cap paid spend until the organic share is ≥40% [ASSUMPTION] |
| (+) | Dependence on state schemes, which change with governments **[S54b]** | Diversify across brands, banks and SHG programmes. Don't build on one scheme |
