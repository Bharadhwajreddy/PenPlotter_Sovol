# Fish Tank: Competitor and Analogue Benchmark

Research date: 7 Nov 2026. Sources: web search and fetches of trade press (Inc42, Entrackr, YourStory, Business Standard, Crowdfund Insider), regulator releases (RBI, FINRA) and company filings (SEC 10-K, annual reports).

**How to read this.** "Unverified" means I could not confirm the number in a reliable source, or the sources disagreed. Data aggregators (Tracxn, Inc42 Datalabs, Latka) often contradict each other or themselves. Where they do, I say so. Status labels are my judgement from the evidence:
- **Succeeding:** growing and profitable, or clearly on that path.
- **Struggling:** shrinking, making losses with no clear path out, under regulatory stress, or pivoting.
- **Failed:** shut down, or closed the relevant business line.

## 1. India: P2P lending (NBFC-P2P)

### Industry context: RBI's August 2024 rule change

- **What changed.** On 16 Aug 2024 RBI amended the NBFC-P2P Master Directions:
  - Platforms may not guarantee returns or principal, offer credit enhancement, or market P2P as an investment product with liquidity or early withdrawal.
  - Escrow money must settle within T+1 days.
  - Platform fees must be fixed or proportional to principal, not tied to repayment.
  - Lenders and borrowers may not be matched inside a "closed user group" of a partner fintech app, which ended the CRED/BharatPe/MobiKwik-style programs.
  - Sources: https://indiacorplaw.in/2024/10/18/rbis-revised-master-directions-on-peer-to-peer-lending-shift-in-regulatory-policy/ and https://blog.thealtinvestor.in/p2p-lending-the-state-of-the-industry-after-rbi-guidelines
- **Volume collapse.** Industry sources told Business Standard (Oct 2024) that volumes fell about 90% after the rules. The same report said 26 players were licensed but only 10–11 were active, and industry AUM was about Rs 11,000 Cr. RBI asked platforms for disbursement data from before and after the change. https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/rbi-asks-p2p-platforms-for-data-on-loan-disbursement-outstanding-loans-124100401077_1.html
- **Enforcement.**
  - Aug 2024: RBI fined LenDenClub (Innofin) Rs 1.99 Cr and LiquiLoans (NDX P2P) about Rs 1.92 Cr, about Rs 3.91 Cr combined. https://www.livelaw.in/news-updates/rbi-imposes-391-crore-penalty-lenden-club-liquiloans-regulatory-violations-p2p-lending-and-digital-practices-267737
  - Oct 2024: show-cause notices went to 6 platforms. https://www.medianama.com/2024/10/223-report-rbi-send-show-cause-notices-to-6-p2p-lending-platforms/
  - Mar 2025: RBI fined 4 more platforms Rs 76.6 lakh in total. https://moneylife.in/article/rbi-imposes-rs7660-lakh-penalty-on-4-nbfcs-for-violating-p2p-lending-regulations/76601.html and RBI release https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=59932
- **New consolidated directions (28 Nov 2025).** RBI consolidated the P2P rules into new 2025 directions. They keep the ban on credit enhancement and guarantees, keep T+1 escrow, and cap a lender's total exposure across all P2P platforms at Rs 50 lakh. https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-non-banking-financial-companies-peer-peer-lending-platform-directions-2025.html
- **Recovery is uneven.** One industry blog said disbursals were still 15–30% of pre-2024 levels. It also said IndiaP2P had returned to pre-2024 volumes, and that Cred Mint and MobiKwik Xtra had to shut their programs. https://blog.thealtinvestor.in/p2p-lending-the-state-of-the-industry-after-rbi-guidelines

## Patterns: what makes these succeed or fail

1. **Any guaranteed return, liquidity or pooling sinks the model.** China P2P, Faircent Double, the 12% Club, LiquiLoans, CRED Mint and MobiKwik Xtra all sold something like a fixed deposit. When regulators acted or defaults rose, they froze or died. RBI now bans this outright.
2. **Retail money is pro-cyclical and leaves first.** Funding Circle's retail investment fell 80% and LendingClub's retail share shrank to a sliver. Zopa cited "loss of trust". The survivors moved to institutional or deposit funding.
3. **The winners become regulated balance sheets, or pure distributors.** LendingClub and Zopa became banks. GetVantage got an NBFC licence. PhonePe distributes loans and leaves the risk to partner NBFCs. Pure marketplaces stay small.
4. **Small-ticket platform fees rarely cover costs.** Mainvest raised $38M in total and still died. StartEngine lost money until pre-IPO SpaceX-style deals produced 79% of its revenue. OkCredit lost about Rs 428 Cr to earn about Rs 9 Cr.
5. **Proprietary cash-flow data beats bank-statement underwriting.** BharatPe and PhonePe lend against payment flows they see and collect from settlements. Lendingkart, which had no such data, saw NPAs jump and was sold at a fraction of its value.
6. **Local partners make small loans repay.** Kiva repays 97% through microfinance field partners but only 72.8% on direct US loans. Rang De relies on NGO partners. Main-street crowdfunding loans (Honeycomb) publish little default data.
7. **Regulatory grey zones get shut down eventually.** Tyke's CSOPs drew RoC fines on issuers. India has no legal retail equity crowdfunding. Wefunder and StartEngine were fined by FINRA. Every Indian P2P platform got fined or shown cause in 2024–25.
8. **Operational single points of failure strand investors.** Mainvest's dependence on Synapse froze $2.4M, and it had no backup servicer. Escrow and servicing continuity matter as much as the product.
9. **Diversified revenue survives regulatory shocks.** LenDenClub rebounded because about 55% of its revenue now comes from LSP and tech services. Single-product P2P players (Faircent, Finzy, i2i) stagnated.
10. **TV-style "deals" are mostly soft.** Only about 41% of Shark Tank India Season 1 deals (27 of 65) and about 42% of committed rupees actually closed. Publicity is the real product.
11. **Free merchant tools win users, not money.** Khatabook and OkCredit reached tens of millions of shopkeepers and still lose money. Monetisation came only where lending sat on top of payments.
12. **Capital-light, profitable early wins the long game.** LenDenClub raised about $12M and earns about Rs 96 Cr PAT. Lendingkart raised $340M+ and was sold at about $100M.

## Implications for Fish Tank

### What to copy

- **LenDenClub's compliant setup:**
  - An RBI-licensed NBFC-P2P partner holds the escrow with T+1 settlement.
  - Lenders choose each loan individually, with explicit consent per loan. Rang De and Finzy were fined for auto-allocation.
  - Fixed, upfront platform fees. Diversified revenue, so a rule change cannot kill the company.
- **PhonePe and BharatPe's data and collection advantage:**
  - Collect from UPI and POS settlements, or via e-NACH/UPI AutoPay, wherever possible.
  - Use live sales data for both underwriting and the "6 months of sales → bank handover". That handover is essentially PhonePe-style loan distribution: a fee-earning, capital-light model that has been proven to work.
- **Kiva's field-partner model:**
  - Use mentors, franchisors and local partners as the "field partner" who vets and supports each business.
  - Paying vendors directly in stages (your plan) is a strong guard against misuse.
- **Franchise India's paying customer:** franchisors pay for qualified, funded franchisees. This is likely Fish Tank's most reliable revenue line. Brinco (Feb 2026) is a new direct competitor to track.
- **Wefunder and Kiva's community storytelling:** video pitches and small tickets are a proven way to get people to engage.

### What to avoid

- **Any wording that implies safe or assured returns or liquidity.** Avoid "earn 12%", "withdraw anytime", "protected", "guaranteed" and secondary-market promises. RBI bans P2P platforms from promoting P2P as an investment product. China, Faircent and the 12% Club show the outcome.
- **Pooling, auto-reinvest across loans, or covering losses from fees.** All are banned, and all were the cause of the 2024–25 fines.
- **Closed-user-group matching inside a partner app.** This is banned. Marketing tie-ups with other apps must not match lenders and borrowers within the partner's own users.
- **"Partnership/equity" and "share of sales" Ask Card options offered to retail investors.**
  - The Tyke precedent: offering securities to more than 200 people outside SEBI rules is illegal private placement, and RoC fined issuers. India has no retail equity crowdfunding regime.
  - Revenue-share or equity may sit outside what an NBFC-P2P loan can be.
  - Get a legal opinion. Likely route: allow only standard loans through the P2P partner, offer equity only to accredited investors via a SEBI angel fund, and treat "guidance only" as non-financial.
- **Lender caps.** Note the Rs 50 lakh total exposure cap per lender across all P2P platforms, and RBI's per-borrower limits. These cap the realistic ask size per business. Check the current directions.
- **Relying on investor Pro subscriptions or small success fees to pay for the platform.**
  - Mainvest, Honeycomb and OkCredit show these do not cover costs at small-business ticket sizes.
  - Lead with franchisor fees and lender referral fees. Keep burn low, as LenDenClub did, rather than following Lendingkart.
- **A single payments or escrow vendor with no backup.** Mainvest and Synapse froze investor money. Contract for a backup servicer and for loans to keep being serviced if Fish Tank itself shuts down.
- **Treating TV-style pitches as closed deals.** Expect fewer than half of public "backings" to complete. Show completion data honestly, and design for staged commitments.
- **Hiding default data.** Honeycomb's credibility gap and Kiva's 72.8% US repayment show that main-street loans default far more than marketing suggests. Publish cohort-level repayment data from day one.
