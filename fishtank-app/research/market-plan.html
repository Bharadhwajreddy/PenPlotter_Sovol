Fish Tank · Market plan · November 2026

Is there a market, and how do we get in?

Built from four research streams: competitor results, market size, go-to-market, and features. Every number comes with a source at the bottom. Numbers marked “assumption” are our own estimates to test.

The verdict

Yes, there is a market. Enter narrow, in two phases, and earn from brands and lenders.

People who need ₹50,000 to ₹10 lakh to start a business are plentiful. Money from ordinary lenders is the scarce side: India’s whole P2P lending industry shrank to about ₹1,500 crore after RBI’s 2024 rules. So Fish Tank launches in Warangal as a free launch platform: pitch, proof, mentors, franchise matching and bank-loan help. Community lending comes later, only after a written legal opinion.

What happened to the competitors

About 30 players benchmarked. The pattern is clear: the ones that make money either connect people to lenders who carry the risk, or became regulated lenders themselves. The ones that promised safe returns or lived on small fees died.

Succeeding

LenDenClub₹96 cr profit in FY26 on only ~$12M raised. 55% of revenue now comes from sourcing loans for others.
PhonePe lendingMerchant loans grew from ₹11 cr to ₹4,507 cr in two years as a pure distributor. Partners carry the risk.
BharatPeEBITDA-positive in FY25. Lends on payment data it already sees.
Franchise India25-year incumbent. Brands pay for events, leads and consulting.
Kiva$2.4B+ lent. 97% repaid via local field partners, only 72.8% on direct US loans.
LendingClub, Zopa, Funding CircleProfitable, but only after leaving retail P2P for bank or institutional money.

Struggling

Faircent, LiquiLoans, Finzy, i2iFundingFined in 2024–25. Pooled, deposit-like products froze.
Rang DeSmall and impact-driven. ₹40 cr+ lent. Fined for auto-allocating lenders.
Khatabook, OkCreditMillions of shopkeepers, but no way to earn from them. OkCredit: ₹428 cr losses vs ₹9 cr revenue.
Tyke, LVX, Grip, Klub, VelocityGrey-zone or loss-making. Startups that raised via Tyke were fined.
Honeycomb Credit, RepublicSub-scale. No public default data.
Shark Tank India (as funding)Only 27 of 65 Season 1 deals closed. The value is publicity.

Failed

BharatPe 12% Club“12%, withdraw anytime” was banned. Folded in Oct 2024.
Mainvest (US)Closed Jun 2024. Small fees, no new funding, $2.4M frozen at a failed payments partner.
LendingkartSold at ~$100M after a $350M+ peak. Bad loans without its own sales data.
China’s P2P industry5,000+ platforms to zero by 2020. Guaranteed returns, pooled money, fraud.
Too early to judgeBrinco (franchise investing, Feb 2026) is the closest new rival. PitchConnectUs and Indian Investment Network: no data found.

Six lessons we act on

  1. Never promise safe returns, liquidity or pooling. Every platform that did was frozen, fined or shut.
  2. Be the connector, not the lender. PhonePe and LenDenClub earn fees while licensed partners carry the risk.
  3. Small fees alone don’t pay for a platform. Mainvest, StartEngine’s crowdfunding arm and OkCredit prove it. Brands and lenders must pay.
  4. Local partners make small loans repay. Kiva’s field partners hit 97%. Our mentors, brands and SHG partners play that role.
  5. Have backups for money rails. Mainvest froze because one payments partner failed. Use two partners.
  6. Publish real repayment data. Hidden defaults destroyed trust for Honeycomb and many others.

Is the market big enough?

Yes for demand. The binding limits are lender money and loss rates, not entrepreneurs. A franchise-only start in one city is too small, so we add shops, food and services in year one.

Revenue in ₹ crore at ₹33,500 per launched business (assumption). Only the optimistic path passes break-even within three years, so costs must stay lean and launches must go beyond franchises.

Lender money is the limit

Optimistic year 3 needs ~₹125 cr a year of lender money, about 8% of today’s whole P2P industry. Banks and MUDRA must stay the main money route.

Losses will be visible

Telangana MUDRA bad loans: 9.4% (Kishor), 6.3% (Tarun). P2P loans 90+ days late: ~20%. Small tickets, staged payouts and published data are a must.

Guidance pays little

The biggest small-town business-education app earns about ₹35 crore from ~1 crore users. Mentoring is a reason to join, not the revenue.

03

What we decide, and do differently

1

Launch without the money feature

RBI’s 2024 rules ban matching lenders and borrowers “within a closed user group”. A platform that brings both sides could fall inside that ban. Day 1 is pitch, proof, mentors, franchise matching and help with MUDRA and JanSamarth loans. Community lending goes live only after a written legal opinion, ideally listed on a P2P partner’s open marketplace.

2

Own the first 180 days

Lenders want 6–24 months of history and new businesses have none. Fish Tank replaces history with checked proof (the Fish Score), then hands each business to a bank after six months of sales. That handover is PhonePe’s proven fee model.

3

Brands and lenders pay, people mostly don’t

Franchise brands pay per outlet opened. Banks pay 0.5–2% per loan referred (benchmark). Business owners pay small fees. Mentors share 20%. No subscription dependence.

4

Start in Warangal, then grow

Franchise aspirants first, then shops, food and services in year one. Vijayawada is city two. Six Tier-2 cities by year three.

5

Speak the user’s language, literally

No lending app does voice-first, Telugu-first business pitching. Meesho’s voice search went from ~10% to ~40% use. This is our clearest product edge.

6

Say “back” or “lend”, never “invest”

No return headlines, no guarantees, no early exit, no public equity offers. Ownership pitches only get “Ask for an introduction”.

04

Features: what we add and what we refuse to build

Add in V3

  • Speak your pitch in Telugu or Hindi. Speaking fills the form.
  • Circle round on WhatsApp: people who know the owner back them first and vouch publicly (if counsel allows).
  • Checked proof and “Why 81?”: each proof needs a photo or document, and the Fish Score is explained.
  • Repayment calendar and UPI AutoPay, with reminders and a clear late-payment policy.
  • Monthly 2-minute update from every funded business. The thing every crowdfunding site lacks.
  • Staged payouts released against a bill and an in-app photo.
  • Spread ₹10,000 across 10 businesses in one tap.
  • Bank loan help: a ready MUDRA application pack and the JanSamarth portal.
  • Every word in Telugu and Hindi, Listen buttons, low-data mode.
  • Fish Tank Live event cards and a ₹100 referral.

Will not build

  • Early exit or a resale market for loans. RBI bars liquidity options.
  • Promised or headline returns. Interest shows only as small print, “not guaranteed”.
  • Public equity crowdfunding. Not legal for ordinary investors in India.
  • Pooled money or auto-reinvest across loans. Each lender chooses each loan.
  • Fish Tank holding money. A licensed partner holds it, with a backup.
  • Paid subscriptions as the main revenue.

The share-of-sales option needs a legal opinion before it’s offered for real money.

How we enter the market: 12 months

Why Warangal

Telangana’s second city, with NIT Warangal’s incubator, SRiX, STPI and an IT hub. It sits inside the state’s women’s-group, Vijaya Dairy, WE Hub and T-Hub programmes. Big enough to matter, small enough to fill.

~1,900 first-time loans a year in our band (estimate)
Who we sign up first

1. Franchise brands (they pay). 2. Would-be franchisees and shop owners. 3. Mentors, as event judges. 4. Banks, for bigger loans. 5. Community lenders, last.

How people hear of us

A monthly Telugu “Fish Tank Live” pitch night cut into reels; a WhatsApp pitch card and city community; ₹100 per verified referral; 2–4 field “Mitras” paid on verified results.

M0–M1

Foundations

  • Legal opinion on P2P, BUDS Act and data rules
  • Interview 20 brands, get letters of intent
  • Build the free tool: Telugu pitch, proof, loan help
M2–M4

Seed Warangal

  • Sign 5–8 brands
  • 2 Fish Tank Live events
  • SHG and college camps
  • Mentors as judges
M5–M7

Turn on money

  • Only with a clean legal opinion
  • One P2P partner, a second as backup
  • ₹50,000 cap per lender per business
  • Bank and MUDRA in parallel
M8–M10

Graduate

  • First businesses with 6 months of sales go to banks
  • Add Karimnagar or Khammam
  • Prepare Vijayawada
M11–M12

Decide

  • Launch Vijayawada only if Gate 3 passes
  • Otherwise deepen Warangal
